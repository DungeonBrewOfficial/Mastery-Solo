# Sites

*Draft 1, 2026-10-24. Tags as in the README. Every [N] stands until corrected.*

Sites are the places worth reaching [Jon, 2026-10-23]: lairs, caves, ruins, barrows, and lost places, turned up by the Discovery table in `04-the-map.md` or found by following a creature's signs. This is where the hoards are, and where Mastery's room-by-room Exploration Experience table finally gets used [M, General Rules, Exploration Experience].

A site is drawn as a **chain of areas** on scrap paper: a circle for each area, a line for each passage. No grid, no measured rooms. Each area is a room, a cave chamber, a stretch of tunnel, or a corner of a ruin, and exploring it takes at least one exploration round [M, General Rules, Exploration Roles].

---

## Questions for Jon

Each is marked **[?]** where it comes up.

1. **Areas instead of rooms on a grid.** A site is a chain of circles and lines, built as you go. It's quick on paper and needs no drawing skill. Measured maps can still be drawn for fights.
2. **Site sizes.** A lair has 2 to 4 areas and a lost place 4 to 14. See Size.
3. **Default DCs by band.** Trials inside a site use the Even and Hard columns of your Setting DCs table [M, Mastery System, Setting DCs]. I'd use the same defaults in the map file, where I'd written 7s.
4. **The clock inside.** Every torch's worth of time (3 exploration rounds) and every fight, a d6 decides whether the site's residents come to see. The longer you stay, the worse it gets.
5. **The guardian's group is spread through the site.** The Occupied areas hold pieces of the guardian's entourage, so clearing the outer areas makes the final fight easier, and skipping them leaves them at your back.
6. **Cleared sites refill.** Return after a month or more, and a cleared site may have new occupants and a smaller hoard.
7. **Magic treasure** (8 XP in Mastery's table) waits on your magic items, so nothing here rolls it yet.
8. **Lost places** use your Asura: the Cherub, a threshold keeper, and the Agathodaimon, a guardian spirit who may not be hostile at all. Is that the right home for them?

## Making a site

1. **Type.** From the Discovery roll, or Lair if you followed signs.
2. **Size.** Roll the number of areas.
3. **Guardian.** Roll who holds the site. The guardian's XP value sets the hoard [`03-treasure.md`, Hoards].
4. **Explore.** Draw the entrance. Each time the party moves into an undrawn area, roll its contents and its passages.
5. **The heart.** The guardian and its hoard are in the last area, unless the contents roll put them somewhere sooner.

## Guardians

Roll for the site's type and the party's band. A guardian from above the party's band is announced as usual: the party finds its signs at the entrance before going in [Jon, 2026-10-24; `02-encounters.md`, Bands]. XP is per character, from the head.

**Lair.** Roll the terrain's encounter table for the party's band (see `02-encounters.md`). This is where that group lives. Reroll Prey. Strange creatures follow their stat blocks; the Magpie's lair is its nest.

## Exploring

Each new area: roll its **contents** (d12), then its **passages** (d6).

### Contents

| d12 | Area | XP [M] |
|:---:|:---|:---:|
| 1–3 | **Empty.** Nothing obvious. An Investigator can search it (see Searching). | 1 |
| 4–5 | **Occupied.** Part of the guardian's group is here (see below). | 2 |
| 6 | **A trap.** | 3 |
| 7 | **A lock or seal** on the way onward. | 3 |
| 8 | **An obstacle or puzzle**: a collapse, a flooded passage, a riddle carved on a door. | 3 |
| 9 | **A cache.** One notable piece [`03-treasure.md`, Filling a hoard]. | 1 |
| 10 | **Remains** of someone who came before: their gear (salvage, at 1d10 × 10% condition), a purse, and a clue. The clue reveals the contents of one undrawn area. | 1 |
| 11 | **A feature** of the site's type (see below). | 1 |
| 12 | **The heart**, if it hasn't been found: the guardian and the hoard. Otherwise, a cache. | 20 (Hoard) |

**Occupied** [N, ?5]: the area holds 1d3 creatures from the guardian's entourage, or one minion group cut to a Cluster. Fight them and they're gone from the final fight. A guardian with no entourage has vermin in these areas instead: a Rat Swarm or a Spider Swarm of the band. XP comes from the strongest creature in the fight, as always.

### Passages

| d6 | Ways on |
|:---:|:---|
| 1–4 | One way on, to the next area. |
| 5 | Two ways on: draw a branch. |
| 6 | No way on: a dead end, unless this would leave areas unreachable, in which case one way on. |

When every area is drawn, any open passages lead to areas already on the map, or back outside. The last area drawn is the heart, if the heart hasn't turned up.

### Default DCs

Trials in a site, unless something sets its own, use your Setting DCs table for the party's level [M, Mastery System, Setting DCs; N, ?3]:

| Band | Ordinary (Even) | Well hidden or well made (Hard) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| I (levels 1–2) | 4 | 6 |
| II (levels 3–5) | 5 | 6 |
| III (levels 6–8) | 7 | 9 |

In Deep country, add 2.

### Searching

An Investigator can search an area for one more exploration round [M, General Rules, Exploration Roles], with an Alertness (Secret Doors or Treasure Sense) trial against the band's Hard DC. On a success, roll a d6: 1–3 nothing, 4–5 hidden treasure (one notable piece; 5 XP), 6 a secret room (one extra area, drawn now, whose contents roll twice and keep the better; 20 XP).

### Traps

Traps use the Set Trap rules, at a tier by band [M, Skill Mastery, Thievery, Set Trap; N]:

| Band | Trap tier | Damage |
|:---:|:---|:---:|
| I | T2, Threatening | 2d3 |
| II | T3, Painful | 2d4 |
| III | T4, Injurious | 3d6 |

In Deep country, one tier higher. The trap's Detection DC is the band's Hard DC. A Scout or Investigator rolls a Perception trial against it to notice the trap, with Alertness (Danger Sense) as the counter [M]. A noticed trap can be avoided, or disabled with Thievery (Breaching) against the same DC, using Breaching's table for what a miss does [M]. An unnoticed trap triggers on the first character through: its damage, and a trial against the Detection DC to resist a condition that fits it (Restricted for a pit, Bleeding for blades, Tainted for a needle, Impaired for a falling weight).

### Locks, seals, and obstacles

A lock is Thievery (Breaching) against the band's Even DC, or Hard for a good one (d6: 5–6) [M]. A stuck or barred door can be forced with a Strength trial at the Hard DC, which is loud: roll the clock. An obstacle is the trial that fits it, at the Even DC: Athletics for a climb or a swim, Lore for a riddle or an inscription, Stone Lore to find a way through a collapse.

### Features by type

| Site | Feature (d6, or choose) |
|:---|:---|
| Lair | Bones and refuse. 1–4: a Throatcutter can harvest parts [M, Exploration Roles]. 5–6: 1d3 pelts of the guardian's prey. |
| Cave | 1–2 underground water, cold and deep. 3–4 a squeeze: anyone in Heavy armor can't pass without taking it off. 5–6 fungus and cave growth: a Forager can gather here. |
| Ruin | 1–2 an unsound floor (an obstacle). 3–4 an old chapel: a holy site [`04-the-map.md`, Spell sources]. 5–6 the remains of a library: occult writings, a source for occult casters. |
| Barrow | Grave goods: one notable piece. Taking them wakes the dead: if the guardian hasn't been met, it comes here next round. |
| Lost place | 1–3 occult writings, a source for occult casters. 4–5 a font, a priscan source. 6 an inscription: Lore (Ancient Lore) at the Hard DC reveals the way to the heart. |

## The clock

Time passes in exploration rounds, and the site's residents don't stay put [N, ?4]. Roll a d6 **every 3 exploration rounds** (the life of one torch [M, Equipment, Torch]), and **after every fight** in the site. On a 6, residents come to see: 1d3 creatures from the guardian's entourage, or vermin of the band. After a fight, a 5 also counts.

Caves, barrows, and the inner parts of ruins are in absolute darkness [M, General Rules, Light and Visibility]. A party without light sees nothing that its own senses can't.

A rest inside a site is a Dungeon Rest at best [M, General Rules, Resting], and roll the clock for it.

---

## The heart

The heart holds the guardian, the rest of its group that hasn't been fought, and the hoard. A beast's hoard is what its victims carried: purses, a dead traveler's pack, a cart's goods, scattered among the bones [N, from the dry run]. The hoard is worth 40 sp per point of the guardian's XP value, doubled in Deep country [`03-treasure.md`]. A site found as a hidden place holds a Hidden Hoard (25 XP instead of 20) [M].

Exploration XP for every area, the hoard, and the site itself (as a New, Hidden, or Lost Location) is banked until the party reaches safety [M].

## Following signs

A Sign from the event die, or tracks found at a site's entrance, can be followed [`01-the-turn.md`]. The Scout makes an Alertness (Stalking) trial against the band's Even DC for a fresh trail, or the Hard DC for an old one [M, Skill Mastery, Alertness]; Wilderness (Hunting and Foraging) or the right Creature Lore gives Dominance. On a success, the trail leads 1d6 squares to the creature's lair: draw it, and it's a site of type Lair with that creature as its guardian. On a failure, the trail is lost for today.

An apex creature's lair is found the same way, and is the most dangerous place on the map.

---

## Coming back

A site stays on the map for the rest of the dynasty. A cleared site may not stay empty [N, ?6]. If the party returns after a month or more, roll a d6: on 1–2, something new has moved in. Roll a new guardian for the site's type and band; its hoard is worth half the usual. On 3–6, the site is still empty, though the clock still applies while the party is inside.
