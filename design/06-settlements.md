# Settlements

*Draft 1, 2026-10-24. Tags as in the README. Every [N] stands until corrected.*

Settlements are where the party comes home to: to rest, repair, buy, sell, and replace the dead. Most of what a settlement offers is already in Mastery, spread across the Availability table, the rest rules, and the price list for lodging and services. This file gathers it by settlement size and adds the two things solo play needs: **recruiting** and **rumors**.

Settlements are placed by the map [`04-the-map.md`, Features]. Once drawn, a settlement stays for the rest of the dynasty.

## Questions for Jon

Each is marked **[?]** where it comes up.

1. **Recruits start at level 1.** Your XP rule makes a newcomer catch up fast, so I haven't offered experienced recruits. The alternative is that towns and cities have recruits a few levels below the party, at the cost of building a leveled character on the spot.
2. **Who's available.** The number of recruits grows with settlement size, and the class roll makes demihumans less common than humans. The class weights are guesses, and a region could reasonably change them.
3. **Hiring a smith.** Your price list has a blacksmith at 8 sp a day. I've given a hired smith a repair rate, and the party still pays for the kits.
4. **Rumors.** An evening at an inn can turn up a rumor of a site somewhere on the blank map. It gives a run somewhere to aim for.
5. **Libraries.** In a city, a library counts as a source of occult writings.
6. **A hamlet's hospitality.** A hamlet has no inn. I've drafted lodging there as a Good Night's Rest if a household takes the party in, which is Mastery's "dwelling with no danger."

**Market dice** decide whether something is for sale [M, Equipment, Availability], and in reverse, whether anyone can afford what the party is selling [`03-treasure.md`, Selling].

**Rest.** A Good Night's Rest needs a tavern, inn, or dwelling with no danger; a Civilized Rest needs a day in a town or city; Bed Rest needs a day in bed somewhere safe [M, General Rules, Resting]. A night at an inn costs 2 cf (Poor), 3 sp (Common), or 6 sp (Good) [M, Equipment, Lodging and Services].

**Holy sites** are spell sources for theurgic casters [M, Magic Mastery, Spell Sources]; roll their form as in `04-the-map.md`, once, and keep it.

**Faction houses** are strongholds of a faith, guild, circle, or order. A character who belongs to that faction can ask for sanctuary: up to three days' stay, food, and the use of its facilities, whether a forge, a library, or a chapel [M, General Rules, Sanctuary].

## Services

**Healing.** A healer treats an Injury for 8 sp [M, Equipment, Lodging and Services]. Traumas take kits and time [M, Combat, Trauma Table; Skill Mastery, Healer]. A Severe Trauma means weeks of rest, so the party either waits in town, paying for lodging, or goes back out without that character.

**Repair** [N, ?3]. A hired smith costs 8 sp a day [M]. The party supplies the repair kits or buys them at the market. A village smith restores 24 Durability per kit, a Skilled smith's average, and works through up to 8 kits a day. A town's workshops can also be rented by a party member with the Blacksmith discipline, which gives Dominance on the work [M, Skill Mastery, Trade Production].

**Selling** uses the market dice in reverse, against the settlement's coin on hand [`03-treasure.md`]. Only a village or larger wants broken armor.

## Recruiting

When a character dies, the party can replace them at a settlement with recruits [Jon, 2026-10-23]. A replacement can be **built** from scratch by the player, who chooses the class, or **recruited** from whoever is available there [Jon, 2026-10-24].

**How many are available.** Roll the Recruits entry for the settlement each time the party visits [N, ?2]. Building a character from scratch doesn't need a recruit; a player who builds is assumed to have found someone.

**Who they are.** For each recruit, roll a d20 for the class [N, ?2]:

| d20 | Class | d20 | Class |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|
| 1–2 | Fighter | 12 | Druid |
| 3 | Knight | 13 | Fili |
| 4 | Ranger | 14 | Dwarf |
| 5–6 | Thief | 15 | Halfling |
| 7 | Cleric | 16 | Gnome |
| 8 | Paladin | 17 | Sylvan Elf |
| 9 | Avenger | 18 | Gray Elf |
| 10 | Magus | 19 | Orc |
| 11 | Bahador | 20 | Cursed |

Then either use a pregen of that class or roll the character up as Mastery's character creation describes.

**Pregens.** A pregen is a ready-made level 1 character of a class. The player can keep a stack of them, one or more per class, and draw from it when a recruit of that class turns up. Your audio roster is a start: Gisla (Cursed) and Corra (Druid) are both level 1 characters built on their own, the way a recruit should be.

**Level.** Recruits start at level 1 with 0 XP [N, ?1]. Because each character takes the full XP of every fight, a newcomer in a party fighting Tier 2 Champions earns 20 XP a fight and reaches level 3 in about 13 fights, while the veterans need far longer for each of their levels. The party's band is set by its average level [Jon, 2026-10-24], so a recruit pulls the band down while they catch up.

**Gear.** A recruit brings their class's starting wealth [M, Equipment, Starting Wealth] and buys their gear at the settlement's market, so a recruit found in a hamlet goes out with hamlet gear.

**Party size.** The baseline party is four [Jon, 2026-10-23].

## Rumors

A party that spends an evening at an inn or alehouse can listen for rumors [N, ?4]. One character makes a Presence trial against the settlement's Even DC [`05-sites.md`, Default DCs]; a roadhouse, full of travelers, gives Dominance. On a success, roll on the Discovery table [`04-the-map.md`] for what the rumor is about, and roll a d8 for its direction and 1d6 for how many tiles away it lies. Mark it on the map lightly: it's a destination, not a discovery, and it pays no XP until the party gets there.

A rumor of a lair can name its creature: roll it on the terrain's encounter table. A rumor of an apex lair is how a party learns there's a dragon in the hills before it ever meets one.

## Home

The dynasty's home village works like any other village until strongholds exist. A dead character's gear left at home stays there for the next recruit.
